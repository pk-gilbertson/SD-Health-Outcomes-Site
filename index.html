Know what the numbers mean

This prototype reorganizes figures already published by the South Dakota Department of Health. It does not replace those reports, estimate anything, or contain record-level data.

Important distinctions

  • Counts, not rates: every measure here is a number of deaths. Rates per 100,000 residents will come once population figures are added. A rising count can come from a larger or older population.
  • All ages versus excluding infants: DOH’s Causes of Death tables leave out deaths of infants under one year old. Its Mortality Reports count all ages. The two are kept as separate series and never joined in one line.
  • Provisional: the latest year in a Mortality Report changes as records arrive. Provisional points are drawn hollow. Earlier releases are kept and shown as revisions.
  • Suppressed: values DOH hides to protect privacy stay hidden. They show as gaps, never zeros.
  • Withheld: values that fail a data check are left off the charts until confirmed. The Data checks view explains each one.
  • Year-to-date tables: monthly reports show January to the current month. They are never treated as full-year totals.

What would make this more useful next?

Population figures would turn counts into rates. County tables would allow maps and comparisons between places. Questions about whether programs work, or how health connects to education and employment, need record-level data shared under data use agreements. That is beyond what public reports can answer.
